Central Event

Oil tanker hit by naval mines in the southern Strait of Hormuz, incident claimed by the Iranian Revolutionary Guard

An oil tanker was hit by two naval mines in the southern portion of the Strait of Hormuz, near the coast of Oman, becoming disabled and on fire according to Iranian state media citing the IRGC. The Guard stated that the vessel was attempting to transit the strait illegally and warned that other ships violating its safety rules will face the same fate, conditioning passage on compliance with Iranian regulations. The incident occurs alongside reports of an exchange of attacks between the US and Iran and the interception of a drone by the United Arab Emirates, still without independent confirmation, signaling a deterioration of the fragile maritime status quo in the region.

Energy Impact

The Strait of Hormuz concentrates 20-30% of globally traded oil. The claimed use of naval mines materially raises the marine insurance risk premium and reintroduces the risk of physical disruption to the Gulf flow. The magnitude of the price effect will depend on the persistence of the threat and any retaliation against infrastructure or additional tankers.

The materialization of a more acute escalation is conditioned on the confirmation of American retaliation or new attacks on tankers; reports of an exchange of attacks between the US and Iran and drone interception by the UAE remain without independent corroboration and should be monitored as risk triggers, not as the basis of the scenario.
